# Remote access: reach your Mac from anywhere (#285)

> [!NOTE]
> **Hidden at launch** (the owner's decision of 2026-10-23, AGENTS.md "The launch build"): Settings › Remote access isn't shown on the Mac or the iPhone, and the iPhone reaches the Mac through the user's own Obysphere instead, sealed end to end ([runner-api.md](runner-api.md), "obid only: the phone relay": obid's side is on `dev`, the iPhone's is in progress). Its code still builds and its tests still pass. Deskree's own Macs show it with a full build ([release.md](release.md), "Full builds": the launch build has no `cloudflared`) and `defaults write com.deskree.oby showEverything -bool YES`, then a relaunch. [Pushes](#pushes), the sealed push relay in the same Worker, isn't hidden.

Outside the home network, the iPhone reaches the Mac with no port forwarding, no VPN app and no Tailscale account: an Oby control plane on Cloudflare gives each installation its own Cloudflare Tunnel, obid on the Mac runs `cloudflared` outbound as its own child (#896, so it outlives the window app), and the iPhone talks HTTPS to an opaque hostname. The pattern is OpenMausBot's `cloudflare/control-plane`.

- **The control plane** is a Worker with a D1 database, `cloud/control-plane/`. Deskree runs one at `oby-control.deskree.com` for its own people, and anyone can run their own ([Instances](#instances-deskrees-and-your-own)). Turning remote access on the first time signs in with Apple or Google, the gate against abuse (the owner's decisions of 2026-10-20): Apple is asked for no name and no email, and the control plane keeps only its `sub`; Google is asked for `openid email`, and the control plane keeps its `sub` and two domains, never the email: `workspace_domain`, Google's `hd`, set only for an account a Google Workspace manages, and `email_domain`, the domain of the email Google verified. They mean different things: only `hd` shows the account belongs to that organization, while a personal Google account made on alice@deskree.com keeps its verified email after Alice leaves. An account is the provider and its `sub`, so an Apple and a Google identity are two accounts, each with the limits below. On an open instance remote access is open to any account, and the domains only bound what one domain's accounts can hold (below), and a members-only instance (`MEMBER_WORKSPACES`) admits only the Workspaces it lists, by `hd` alone. Each Mac makes a P-256 key, registers an installation by its public key with that sign-in, and signs every call with it. The iPhone only scans the Mac's QR code.
- **Sign in with Apple is on the web, not native:** Apple's [supported capabilities for macOS](https://developer.apple.com/help/account/reference/supported-capabilities-macos) lists Sign in with Apple for App Store apps only, not Developer ID, and a Developer ID profile never carries `com.apple.developer.applesignin`. So the Mac app has no entitlement for it: it opens Apple's sign-in page in the system's web sheet (`ASWebAuthenticationSession`, sharing Safari's cookies, so an Apple Account already signed in, or a passkey, makes it quick), Apple posts the identity token to the control plane, and the control plane checks it (Apple's keys, `iss`, `aud` = the Services ID, `exp`, `iat`, the single-use nonce it handed out for that Mac's key, `sub`). The token never passes through the Mac. The control plane sends the browser back to `oby-signin://done` with the state and a one-time code (kept only as a hash), and the Mac registers with both: someone who starts a sign-in on their own Mac and gets another person to finish it at Apple has the state, never the code, so they can't take that person's account.
- **Sign in with Google works the same way:** a signed `POST /v1/auth/google/start` stores the state, the nonce's hash and the key, and answers Google's authorization URL (`response_type=id_token`, `response_mode=form_post`, `scope=openid email`, `prompt=select_account`); the same web sheet opens it, Google posts the ID token to `/v1/auth/google/callback`, and the control plane checks it (Google's keys from `https://www.googleapis.com/oauth2/v3/certs`, kept and refetched as Apple's are; RS256; `iss` `https://accounts.google.com` or `accounts.google.com`; `aud` = `GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID`; `exp`; `iat`; the nonce; `sub`; `email_verified` true), then sends the browser back to `oby-signin://done` with the state and a one-time code, exactly as for Apple. The ID token flow needs no client secret: the control plane never exchanges a code. A sign-in started for one provider is finished only by that provider's callback.
- **Abuse control.** The gate is the account (Apple or Google); the rest bounds what one account, one network, or everyone can cost:
  - **per account** (Apple or Google): at most 5 Macs, 5 live endpoints, 10 Macs set up a day, and 10 endpoints made a day. An account with 5 Macs that signs in on a sixth loses the one it used longest ago (its tunnel too), and the new Mac's Settings says which; if tearing that one down fails, nothing changes, the try isn't counted, and the sign-in works again. That's not a way back from a stolen account: an attacker who keeps calling stays the most recently used, so the owner's own Macs would go first. A stolen account is recovered at Apple or Google; here it reaches only remote access (a hostname to a gateway that still needs a paired device's token), never a token or data. Counting, then inserting, isn't atomic, so two registrations at once can leave an account one Mac over; the daily limits bound it. An installation without an account is refused, never spared the limits;
  - **per domain,** for Google accounts: every account under one registrable domain shares an allowance (`users.limit_domain`, so `a.evil.com` and `b.evil.com` are one). Google accounts are cheap in bulk: a domain on free Cloud Identity, or a catch-all domain, gives hundreds of `sub`s on as many subdomains as you like, each with its own per-account limits, enough to fill `MAX_ENDPOINTS` without this.
    - A **Workspace account** (`hd`) counts under its Workspace's registrable domain, with `WORKSPACE_DOMAIN_ENDPOINTS` live endpoints (100) and `WORKSPACE_DOMAIN_REGISTRATIONS` Macs set up a day (50), vars in `wrangler.jsonc` the owner raises for a bigger company (a value that isn't a whole number refuses, 503, rather than lifting the limit).
    - **Any other Google account** counts under its verified email's registrable domain, with 25 live endpoints and 20 Macs set up a day, fixed. Against the same count: a personal account on a company's addresses is refused past 25 even when the company's Workspace may have 100, and its refused tries don't spend the Workspace's daily allowance (the count is looked at before it's taken).
    - **Public mail providers aren't limited together:** one shared bucket for, say, `outlook.com` would let a stranger lock its users out. The list (`PUBLIC_MAIL_DOMAINS` in `src/app.ts`) is exact registrable domains, never a pattern, so a look-alike such as `yahoo.xyz` isn't exempt: Gmail, Outlook, Hotmail, Live, MSN, Yahoo (and its regional domains), iCloud, Proton, AOL, GMX, WEB.DE, Yandex, Mail.ru, QQ, 163 and 126. **Residual risk:** a small free provider not on the list is one bucket, so a stranger could fill its 25 and lock its other users out of remote access; add it to the list when one turns up. Registrable domains come from the [Public Suffix List](https://publicsuffix.org)'s ICANN section, bundled as `src/suffixes.ts` (`pnpm suffixes` regenerates it from the live list; commit the result), so `x.ac.in` and `y.ac.in` are two buckets. Its private section (`github.io`, `workers.dev`…) isn't used: names anyone can get under a service stay one domain to the limits.
    - Apple accounts have no domain. A Mac set up past the allowance is refused (429) and one turning remote access on past it gets 409; the live count is read before Cloudflare is called, as the account's is;
  - on an open instance the hostnames live on **a domain of their own** (`DEVICE_DOMAIN`), never under `PRODUCT_DOMAIN`, so a phishing page on one can't get the product's domain flagged by Safe Browsing or set cookies on it; only a members-only instance may share it ([Instances](#instances-deskrees-and-your-own));
  - **a backstop per network,** generous so offices, carrier-grade NAT and mobile networks aren't held back: 30 sign-ins started and 20 Macs set up an hour per client (an IPv6 /64 is one), 100 Macs set up an hour per network (an IPv4 /24 or IPv6 /48), 30 endpoints made an hour and 100 a day per network, and at most 20 endpoints not yet connected per network (connected ones don't count);
  - **live endpoints:** at most `MAX_ENDPOINTS` (500, under the account's tunnel limit). The cap is read before the Cloudflare calls and the row written after them, so calls in flight at once can overshoot it by a few. Past 80% of the cap, every new endpoint logs `endpoints: N of M alive, past 80% of MAX_ENDPOINTS` (Workers Logs): set an alert on that line;
  - **a hostname is kept by keeping the tunnel connected:** an endpoint whose tunnel never connected within an hour is reclaimed, and so is one whose tunnel has been down for 72 hours; the installation stays either way, so a Mac behind a firewall that stops cloudflared keeps its sign-in (its check-in makes a new endpoint, up to 3 a day). Cloudflare sets `conns_inactive_at` even for a tunnel that never connected, so "never connected" means no `conns_active_at`. So a Mac that's off, asleep, or has remote access off for three days gives its hostname up; when it turns on again it gets a new one, and the iPhone pairs again;
  - **Cloudflare calls,** which share the account's one API token: each installation makes or removes its tunnel at most 5 times an hour, makes one at most 3 times a day, and reads its token at most 4 times an hour; over everyone, 300 new tunnels and 120 removals an hour, and 300 token reads every 5 minutes. Draining the 300 an hour takes 30 accounts;
  - **the sweep,** every minute: spent nonces, sign-ins and day-old rate-limit windows go; installations that made no tunnel in a day go, all at once; installations no Mac signed a call for in 30 days go with their tunnels, 8 a run; and 8 endpoints, never-checked first (oldest made first), then the longest ago checked, are asked about at Cloudflare (480 an hour, more than the 300 new tunnels an hour, and all 500 checked about once an hour). That's at most 40 Cloudflare calls a run, under the free plan's 50.
  
  **No proof of work any more.** With an Apple or Google account as the gate, a proof of work added a second or two to every new Mac and stopped little: it costs a GPU almost nothing, while each account is limited above and new Apple and Google accounts usually need a phone number. **What's left:** someone with many accounts and networks could still fill the endpoint cap, by keeping 500 tunnels connected. Apple's server-to-server notifications (an account deleted, or consent revoked for Oby) aren't handled yet: such an account's Macs stay until they're forgotten, idle for 30 days, or their tunnel is down for 3 days. They'd need the notification endpoint registered for the App ID in Certificates, Identifiers & Profiles, and a route that checks Apple's signed payload with the same keys. Google's Cross-Account Protection (RISC) events, the same for Google accounts, aren't handled either.
- **Hostnames** are first-level names under the device domain, `oby-<20 opaque characters>.<DEVICE_DOMAIN>`, so Cloudflare's free Universal SSL covers them. Never a second-level name: that would need Advanced Certificate Manager. The device domain is a registrable domain of its own: the Worker compares registrable domains (eTLD+1, by the Public Suffix List's ICANN section, `src/suffixes.ts`) and refuses one that shares `PRODUCT_DOMAIN`'s unless `MEMBER_WORKSPACES` is set, fails closed when `PRODUCT_DOMAIN` is empty, and checks once that `CF_ZONE_ID`'s zone is `DEVICE_DOMAIN`. `MAX_ENDPOINTS` must be a whole number, or the Worker refuses to make endpoints until it is; add it to the [Public Suffix List](https://publicsuffix.org/submit/) so browsers treat each hostname as its own site. The Worker never touches records it didn't make.
- **The tunnel** routes one hostname to the gateway on the Mac (`http://127.0.0.1:8810`, #284) and nothing else: the last ingress rule is `http_status:404`.
- **Apple's and Google's keys** are each kept for a day and fetched again for an unknown `kid` at most once a minute. A fetch that fails or answers something other than a list of RSA keys changes nothing: the kept keys stay, and the next try waits 1, 2, 4… minutes, up to an hour, while the provider is down.
- **Secrets** live only in the Worker's secrets (the Cloudflare API token, the account and zone ids) and in the Mac's keychain (the installation's key, the tunnel's token). Sign in with Apple and Sign in with Google need no secret: the control plane only checks the identity token with the provider's public keys, and never exchanges an authorization code. D1 keeps public keys, each account's provider and `sub` (a Google account's key is `google:<sub>`; a `sub` never holds a colon, so the two can't meet), a Google account's Workspace and email domains, the hash of each sign-in's nonce, and spent nonces; never an email, and nothing secret is logged.

## Instances: Deskree's, and your own

Oby is open source, and the control plane is a service someone has to run. Each running copy is an **instance**: a Worker, its D1 database, its sign-in clients and its domains. An instance is a named environment in `cloud/control-plane/wrangler.jsonc` (`env.<name>`); the file's top level is a template with nothing instance-specific, which on its own signs nobody in. Nothing about an instance is in the code: every setting is a var (documented in `src/env.ts`) or a secret.

| | Deskree's instance (`env.deskree`) | Yours |
|---|---|---|
| Who may sign in | Deskree's Google Workspace accounts only (`MEMBER_WORKSPACES = deskree.com`) | Anyone with an Apple or Google account (`MEMBER_WORKSPACES` empty), or your own Workspaces |
| The Worker's hostname | `oby-control.deskree.com` | `oby-control.<your domain>` |
| The Macs' hostnames | `oby-<opaque>.deskree.com` (allowed only because it's members-only) | `oby-<opaque>.<a domain of their own>` |
| The Mac app points at it with | `OBY_CONTROL_PLANE_URL` in `apps/config/Signing.xcconfig` (the default) | the same setting, in your `apps/config/Signing.local.xcconfig` or your fork's `Signing.xcconfig` |

**Why the Macs' hostnames want a domain of their own.** On an open instance, anyone who signs in gets a hostname. If those hostnames were under the product's domain, one person's phishing page on `oby-….example.com` could get all of `example.com` flagged by Safe Browsing, and a page there could set cookies for the product's site. So an open instance refuses a `DEVICE_DOMAIN` that shares `PRODUCT_DOMAIN`'s registrable domain. A **members-only** instance (`MEMBER_WORKSPACES` set) may share it: only accounts the listed Google Workspaces manage (Google's `hd`, never just an email address on that domain) sign in or register a Mac, so only your own people ever get a name there. Apple accounts have no Workspace, so a members-only instance refuses Sign in with Apple. Membership is checked at sign-in and again when the Mac registers.

## Run your own control plane (the user's step)

Everything below runs on your Mac, in `cloud/control-plane/`. Deploying is never an agent's job. `<name>` is your environment's name, `<domain>` the domain your Cloudflare account holds.

1. **Add your environment.** In `wrangler.jsonc`, copy the `deskree` block inside `env`, rename it (say `acme`), and set its values: the route `oby-control.<domain>`, `PRODUCT_DOMAIN`, `DEVICE_DOMAIN`, `MEMBER_WORKSPACES`, and the redirect URIs (`https://oby-control.<domain>/v1/auth/apple/callback` and `…/google/callback`). Wrangler doesn't inherit `vars` or `d1_databases` from the top level, so the block lists them all. Only public values go in this file: it's in git.
2. **Choose the device domain.** Members-only: it may be `<domain>` itself. Open to anyone: register a domain for the Macs' hostnames alone, add it to Cloudflare as its own zone, set `DEVICE_DOMAIN` to it, and submit it to the [Public Suffix List](https://publicsuffix.org/submit/) (its private section), so each `oby-…` hostname is its own site to browsers. Until `DEVICE_DOMAIN` is set, the Worker makes no endpoint.
3. **Set up Sign in with Google**, in your Google Cloud project ([console.cloud.google.com](https://console.cloud.google.com), Google Auth Platform):
   - **Branding (the OAuth consent screen):** app name "Oby", your support email, the authorized domain `<domain>`. **Audience:** *Internal* for a members-only instance (only your Workspace's accounts can even try), or *External* and then **Publish app** for an open one (while it's in Testing, only the listed test users can sign in). **Data access (scopes):** `openid` and `.../auth/userinfo.email` only; both are non-sensitive, so Google's app verification isn't needed.
   - **Clients › Create client:** type **Web application**, name "Oby remote access"; **Authorized redirect URIs:** `https://oby-control.<domain>/v1/auth/google/callback`. No JavaScript origin is needed. Copy the **Client ID** (`….apps.googleusercontent.com`) into your block's `GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID` (public). Google also shows a client secret: the control plane never exchanges a code, so don't put it anywhere. Until `GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID` is set, Sign in with Google answers 503.
4. **Set up Sign in with Apple** (open instances only; members-only refuses it), in Certificates, Identifiers & Profiles on your team:
   - **Identifiers › your Mac app's App ID:** tick **Sign in with Apple** and save (as a primary App ID). The Mac app gets no entitlement for it, so its Developer ID signing and profile stay as they are.
   - **Identifiers › + › Services IDs:** a Services ID (say `<your bundle id>.signin`); tick **Sign in with Apple** › Configure: primary App ID your app's; Domains `oby-control.<domain>`; Return URLs `https://oby-control.<domain>/v1/auth/apple/callback`. Save. No file goes on the server and no key is needed.
   - `APPLE_CLIENT_ID` is that Services ID and `APPLE_REDIRECT_URI` that return URL; neither is a secret.
5. **Install the tools:** `pnpm install` (npm works too). `pnpm test` runs the Worker's tests in Miniflare, `pnpm check` type-checks.
6. **Sign wrangler in:** `pnpm exec wrangler login`. This OAuth login deploys the Worker and its D1 database. If it can see more than one Cloudflare account, set `CLOUDFLARE_ACCOUNT_ID` in your shell for the commands below rather than writing the id in `wrangler.jsonc`.
7. **Create the API token the Worker uses at run time**, in the Cloudflare dashboard, My Profile › API Tokens › Create Token › Custom token, with exactly:
   - Account › **Cloudflare Tunnel › Edit**, on your account;
   - Zone › **DNS › Edit**, on the device domain's zone only.

   Nothing else: the token makes tunnels and CNAME records and reads tunnel tokens. (Pushes need an APNs key of your own team's: [Pushes, Setting it up](#setting-it-up-the-owners-step).) (A CI machine that deploys instead of `wrangler login` needs Workers Scripts › Edit and D1 › Edit as well, in a token of its own.)
8. **Find the ids:** the account id and the device domain's zone id are on that zone's Overview page in the dashboard, right column. `CF_ZONE_ID` is the device domain's zone.
9. **Create the database:** `pnpm exec wrangler d1 create oby-control`, then paste the `database_id` it prints into your block's `d1_databases`.
10. **Run the migrations:** `pnpm migrate:remote --env <name>`.
11. **Set the secrets**, one at a time; each asks for the value on stdin, so nothing lands in your shell history or in git:
    ```sh
    pnpm exec wrangler secret put CF_API_TOKEN --env <name>
    pnpm exec wrangler secret put CF_ACCOUNT_ID --env <name>
    pnpm exec wrangler secret put CF_ZONE_ID --env <name>
    ```
12. **Deploy:** `pnpm run deploy --env <name>` (`pnpm exec wrangler deploy --dry-run --env <name>` first shows every setting it will use). The route is a custom domain, so Cloudflare adds the `oby-control.<domain>` record and certificate itself, and the cron (every minute) starts sweeping. Then, in Workers Logs, alert on the line `past 80% of MAX_ENDPOINTS`.
13. **Check it:** `curl https://oby-control.<domain>/v1/health` answers `{"ok":true}`.
14. **Point your Mac app at it:** `OBY_CONTROL_PLANE_URL = https:/$()/oby-control.<domain>` in `apps/config/Signing.local.xcconfig` (the `$()` keeps an xcconfig from reading `//` as a comment), then build Oby. Remote access in Settings now signs in with your instance.

To rotate the API token later, make a new one with the same scopes, `wrangler secret put CF_API_TOKEN --env <name>` again, and delete the old one in the dashboard. Nothing on the Macs changes.

**Deskree's own instance** is the steps above with `<name>` = `deskree` and `<domain>` = `deskree.com`, members-only, so step 4 is skipped and the Google client's Audience is Internal.

## Working on the Worker locally

```sh
cd cloud/control-plane
cp .dev.vars.example .dev.vars    # placeholders; fill in what you have
pnpm migrate:local                # the D1 database in .wrangler/
pnpm dev --env <name>             # http://localhost:8787, with that instance's vars
pnpm test   # inside workerd (Miniflare), with the Cloudflare API mocked
```

Without the three `CF_*` secrets, registering still works; asking for an endpoint answers 503 saying what's missing. Signing in needs Apple, so locally a sign-in can't finish; the tests use a fake Apple and a fake Google (`test/helpers.ts`, `FakeIdP`) that sign tokens with their own keys. `wrangler dev --test-scheduled` runs the sweep at `/__scheduled`.

### The API

Every answer is JSON; an error is `{"error": "one line to show"}` with its status. There are no bearer tokens: every call but `/v1/health`, `/v1/auth`, the providers' callbacks, a hook's `/h/{id}` and the push relay's routes ([Pushes](#pushes)) is signed by the Mac's key, with these headers:

- `Oby-Key`: the public key, an uncompressed P-256 point (X9.63, 65 bytes), base64url with no padding. One spelling per key.
- `Oby-Timestamp`: Unix seconds; refused when more than 5 minutes from the Worker's clock (401, "This Mac's clock is off…").
- `Oby-Nonce`: 16 to 64 letters, digits, `-` or `_`; each one works once per key (401, "That call was made already").
- `Oby-Signature`: ECDSA P-256 with SHA-256, r‖s (64 bytes), base64url, over `oby-control-v1`, the method, the path, the timestamp and the nonce, each followed by a newline, then the body's bytes.

A bad or missing signature is 401; a good signature from a key with no installation is 410 ("This Mac isn't registered"), apart from a 404 for no such route, and tells the Mac to register again. A nonce is spent only once the call's installation is found (or its sign-in, or the sign-in it starts), so keys with no installation leave nothing in D1 when they call anything else.

| Call | What |
| --- | --- |
| `GET /v1/auth` | Not signed. What a Mac shows before it signs in: `{providers, workspaces}`, the providers set up and allowed (`google`, `apple`; a members-only instance leaves Apple out) and the Workspaces a members-only instance is for. |
| `POST /v1/auth/apple/start` | Signed. Starts a sign-in for the signing key: `{url, state}` (201), `url` being Apple's authorization page with `client_id` (the Services ID), `redirect_uri`, `response_type=code id_token`, `response_mode=form_post`, the `state` and a fresh `nonce`, and no `scope`, so Apple sends no name or email. The state works for 10 minutes; D1 keeps the nonce's SHA-256 only. 30 an hour per client. |
| `POST /v1/auth/apple/callback` | Apple's form post (not signed: Apple sends it). Checks the identity token against the sign-in its `state` names, binds the account's `sub` to it with a one-time code, and sends the browser to `oby-signin://done?state=…&code=…`, or `oby-signin://failed?state=…` when the token doesn't check out, the user cancelled, the sign-in expired or was used. 60 an hour per client. |
| `POST /v1/auth/google/start` | Signed. The same for Google: `url` is Google's authorization page (`https://accounts.google.com/o/oauth2/v2/auth`) with `client_id` (`GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID`), `redirect_uri`, `response_type=id_token`, `scope=openid email`, `prompt=select_account`, `response_mode=form_post`, the `state` and a fresh `nonce`. 503 until `GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID` is set. Counted with Apple's sign-ins. |
| `POST /v1/auth/google/callback` | Google's form post. Checks the ID token (above) against the sign-in its `state` names, which must have been started for Google; keeps the account as `google:<sub>` with its Workspace and email domains, and redirects as Apple's does. Counted with Apple's callbacks. |
| `POST /v1/installations` `{name, platform, signIn, signInCode}` | Registers the signing key (201) or, the same key again, keeps its installation and hostname (200, no sign-in needed); `{installation, evicted?}`. A new key needs `signIn` and `signInCode`: the state of a sign-in that key started and the code its sheet came back with, used once (403 otherwise, "Sign in with Apple or Google first."). The installation belongs to that account: 5 Macs an account (a sixth takes the place of the one used longest ago, named in `evicted`), 10 set up a day, 20 a day per Google email domain or 50 per Workspace (`WORKSPACE_DOMAIN_REGISTRATIONS`); 20 an hour per client and 100 per network. |
| `GET /v1/installations/self` | This installation, with its endpoint and `account` (`{provider, domain}`: the domain kept for a Google account, null for Apple; never an email). Like every signed call, it keeps the installation from being swept as idle. |
| `DELETE /v1/installations/self` | Forget This Mac: tears the tunnel and record down at Cloudflare, then removes the installation. If Cloudflare refuses, nothing is removed and the call fails. |
| `POST /v1/installations/self/endpoint` | Refuses (503) without a proper `DEVICE_DOMAIN` or at `MAX_ENDPOINTS`, (409) when the account has 5 live endpoints, or its Google email domain 25 or its Workspace 100 (`WORKSPACE_DOMAIN_ENDPOINTS`), and (429) with 20 unconnected endpoints from this network already; 5 tunnel changes an hour and 3 creations a day per installation, 10 a day per account, 30 an hour and 100 a day per network, 300 new tunnels an hour over everyone. Makes the tunnel (`config_src: cloudflare`), its ingress (the hostname to the gateway, then `http_status:404`) and the proxied CNAME to `<tunnel>.cfargotunnel.com`; `{endpoint: {hostname, tunnelId, createdAt}}`. Again later: the same one. If the record fails, the tunnel is removed and the call fails. |
| `GET /v1/installations/self/endpoint/token` | `{token, hostname}`: the tunnel's run token, read from Cloudflare each time, never stored. 4 an hour per installation, 300 every 5 minutes over everyone. |
| `DELETE /v1/installations/self/endpoint` | Removes the record and the tunnel. Counted with the installation's tunnel changes, and 120 removals an hour over everyone (Forget This Mac too). |
| `POST /v1/installations/self/hooks` | Signed. A hook address for this Mac (#1128): `{hook: {id, url, createdAt}}` (201), `id` 32 lower-case letters and digits (about 165 bits), `url` `https://<the Worker's host>/h/<id>`. At most 20 per installation (409) and 20 made a day (429). |
| `GET /v1/installations/self/hooks` | Signed. `{hooks: [ … ]}`, this installation's only. |
| `DELETE /v1/installations/self/hooks/{id}` | Signed. 204, or 404 for a hook that isn't this installation's. |
| `POST /h/{id}` | Public: a sender's delivery (GitHub, Grafana, Alertmanager, PagerDuty, …), POST only (405). An unknown hook is 404, whether or not it was ever made. A body over 1 MiB is 413, read as a stream and cut off. 60 a minute and 2,000 a day per hook (429); the day counts only what obid didn't refuse as unsigned (401), and what's kept, so a stranger's junk can't use it up. Passed to `https://<hostname>/v1/hooks/<id>/deliver` with the body as it came and only `Content-Type`, `X-Hub-Signature-256`, `X-GitHub-Event`, `X-GitHub-Delivery`, `X-Oby-Signature-256`, `X-Oby-Token`, `X-Grafana-Alerting-Signature`, `X-Grafana-Alerting-Timestamp` and `X-PagerDuty-Signature`: never a cookie, `Authorization` or a Cloudflare header of the sender's. The Worker waits 10 seconds and answers obid's status with a fixed body of its own (`{"ok": …}`), so nothing obid says reaches the sender. A Mac with no endpoint, a tunnel that doesn't answer, or a 5xx is the Mac away: the delivery is kept sealed (below) and answered 202 `{"ok": true, "message": "Kept for that Oby."}`, so the sender doesn't retry into a growing pile; with no queue key, or past a bound, nothing is kept and it's 503 (`That Oby isn't reachable now.`). |
| `PUT /v1/installations/self/queue-key` `{key}` | Signed. obid's queue key (#1315): a P-256 public key, X9.63, base64url (422 otherwise), kept beside the installation's own. From then on a delivery while this Mac is away is kept sealed to it. 204. |
| `GET /v1/installations/self/hooks/queue` | Signed. What was kept for this installation, oldest first, 20 at a time: `{items: [{id, hook, receivedAt, sealed}], left}`, `sealed` base64url, `left` how many more. `Cache-Control: no-store`. |
| `DELETE /v1/installations/self/hooks/queue/{id}` | Signed. The Mac handed one to obid: it goes. 204, or 404 for one that isn't this installation's. |

**Kept deliveries** (#1315). The Worker keeps what it can't read: when `POST /h/{id}` can't reach the Mac, the delivery (`{"receivedAt", "headers", "body"}`, the forwarded headers only) is sealed to the installation's queue key (an ephemeral P-256 key, ECDH, HKDF-SHA-256 with info `oby-hook-queue/1`, AES-256-GCM with a random nonce and the hook's id as associated data; runner-api.md, "obid only: webhooks"), and D1's `hook_queue` keeps only the sealed bytes, the hook, the installation, when and how big. Only obid, whose vault holds the private half, can open it; the Worker can't check the sender's signature either, so what it keeps is unverified until obid opens it and checks it as a live one. Bounds, each answered 503 with nothing kept: a body over 256 KiB, 100 items or 2 MiB per installation, and `QUEUE_TOTAL_MIB` (wrangler.jsonc, 200 by default, under D1's 500 MB a database on the free plan) for everyone, with a warning in Workers Logs past 80%. The per-hook rates come first. Logs name hooks and sizes only. The Mac app hands them to obid: while remote access is on, whenever obid comes up (at launch, after a restart or sleep) or the tunnel connects, and every 5 minutes after, it registers obid's queue key (when it changed) and collects; each is removed once obid answered anything but a 5xx or a 429 (one the sweep took meanwhile is passed over). Items older than 72 hours go in the sweep; removing a hook or the installation removes its items.

The cron (`sweep` in `src/app.ts`, every minute) deletes spent nonces, day-old rate-limit windows and deliveries kept 72 hours, removes installations that made no tunnel in 24 hours (and what they kept, and their hooks) in one statement, removes up to 8 installations idle for 30 days, tunnels and records first, and asks about 8 endpoints (never-checked first): one whose tunnel never connected within an hour, or has been down for 72 hours, is reclaimed (the installation stays, and isn't swept as never used: it made an endpoint once); one that Cloudflare refuses is tried again next time.

## Pushes

How Oby reaches the iPhone when the phone isn't talking to the Mac (#398, the owner's decision of 2026-10-23, which replaced that issue's first plan): through a relay in this Worker that only ever sees sealed blobs. Apple's push key is Deskree's and can't ship inside Oby, so obid can't call APNs itself; the relay holds the key, and the alerts stay between obid and the phone. This is contract v2 (the coordinator's review of 2026-10-23): obid never holds the phone's `pushSecret`, and every push carries its time.

1. The iPhone registers its APNs device token with the relay, with no sign-in. The relay checks the token with Apple, then answers a `pushId` and a `pushSecret`.
2. The phone gives its obid the `pushId`, the `pushKey` made from the secret, and the public key alerts are sealed to (obid's side: docs/runner-api.md). It keeps the `pushSecret` to itself: only the secret re-registers or forgets the phone.
3. For each alert, obid seals it to the phone's key and posts the sealed blob to the relay with its time, signed with an HMAC under the `pushKey`.
4. The relay checks the HMAC and the time, and sends Apple a fixed alert carrying the blob. The phone's Notification Service Extension opens the blob and shows the real alert; one it can't open shows the fixed words, "Oby: Open Oby to see it."

These routes are the one part of the control plane open to any phone, outsiders included, on every instance: `MEMBER_WORKSPACES` doesn't apply, and the phone never signs in.

**The keys.** Every HMAC is HMAC-SHA-256, and **every HMAC key is raw bytes, never base64url text**: a `pushSecret`'s or a `pushKey`'s raw bytes are its base64url decoded, 32 bytes. Every result is base64url with no padding (43 characters).

- `pushSecret` = HMAC(key: the UTF-8 bytes of the relay's own `PUSH_SECRET_KEY`, message: UTF-8 `oby-push-secret/1`, a newline, then the `pushId`). The relay makes it again whenever it needs it, so D1 keeps no secret. Only the relay makes it.
- `pushKey` = HMAC(key: the 32 raw bytes of `pushSecret`, message: UTF-8 `oby push key v1`). The phone makes it, and the relay makes it again from the `pushSecret`.
- `Oby-Push-Mac` = HMAC(key: the 32 raw bytes of `pushKey`, message: the raw request body bytes, exactly as sent).
- A `pushSecret` or `Oby-Push-Mac` that isn't exactly the relay's own 43 base64url characters (32 bytes, no padding) is refused (401), compared in constant time.

A fixed vector to check against (`test/push.test.ts`, "the keys", worked out apart from the relay with Python's `hmac`):

### The calls

| Call | What |
| --- | --- |
| `POST /v1/push/devices` `{"apns", "topic", "sandbox"}` | From the iPhone, `Content-Type: application/json` only (415 otherwise). A web page can't send that without a CORS preflight, which this Worker refuses, so a page can't register tokens from its visitors' addresses. `apns` is the device token in hex (64 to 200 characters, kept lower-case); `topic` the app's bundle id, one of `APNS_TOPICS` (`com.deskree.oby.ios` on Deskree's instance); `sandbox` true for a development build (APNs' sandbox), false for TestFlight and the App Store. **A token new to the relay is checked with Apple before it's kept:** one background push (`apns-push-type: background`, priority 5, `{"aps": {"content-available": 1}}`, nothing shown). If Apple answers 410, `BadDeviceToken` or `DeviceTokenNotForTopic` (a wrong `sandbox` too), the answer is 422 and nothing is kept; if Apple doesn't answer, 502. Then 200 `{"pushId", "pushSecret"}`, `Cache-Control: no-store`: `pushId` is 24 base64url characters (18 random bytes), `pushSecret` 43. A token another `pushId` already has moves to this one, and the other is forgotten. Any other field is 422, a body over 1 KiB 413. Limits: 30 new phones an hour per client and 200 per network (429), 2,000 an hour over everyone, 10 checks an hour per token, and `MAX_PUSH_DEVICES` registered at once (503, with a warning in Workers Logs past 80%; the count is one row kept by triggers, so a registration reads one row, not the table). |
| `POST /v1/push/devices` `{"apns", "topic", "sandbox", "pushId", "pushSecret"}` | The phone registers a changed token for the same id: 200 with the same `pushId` and `pushSecret`. A changed token is checked with Apple first; the same token again isn't. It brings back an id the relay forgot (after Apple said the token was bad), since the secret proves it's that phone's, so the phone may call it at every launch. A wrong secret (the `pushKey` too), an id never made, or one of the two missing: 401, the same answer each time; the phone then registers anew and gives obid the new `pushId` and `pushKey`. 20 an hour per `pushId`. |
| `DELETE /v1/push/devices/{pushId}` | With `Authorization: Bearer <pushSecret>`. Forgets the phone: 204, also when it was gone already; 401 for a wrong or missing secret, the `pushKey` included, so obid can't. |
| `POST /v1/push` `{"pushId", "sealed", "collapse"?, "urgent"?, "at"}` | From obid, with `Oby-Push-Mac` (above). `at` is the push's time in Unix seconds, required; the MAC covers it, and more than 5 minutes from the relay's clock either way is 401. (The phone drops repeats and stale alerts by the sealed alert's id and time.) `sealed` is base64url, at most 3,072 characters (413), so Apple's payload stays under its 4 KB; `collapse` 1 to 64 printable ASCII characters with no space (it reaches Apple as `apns-collapse-id`, so make it opaque); `urgent` true or false, false when it's missing. Any other field is 422, a body over 4 KiB 413. A missing or wrong MAC is 401, the same for an id never made, and it's checked before D1 is read or anything is counted. Then 30 a minute and 1,000 a day per `pushId`, and the same per token, so one phone registered under new pushIds shares one allowance (429). The relay sends `POST https://api.push.apple.com/3/device/<token>` (`api.sandbox.push.apple.com` for a sandbox phone) with `apns-topic`, `apns-push-type: alert`, `apns-priority` 10 when urgent and 5 otherwise, `apns-collapse-id` when given, and `{"aps": {"alert": {"title": "Oby", "body": "Open Oby to see it."}, "mutable-content": 1, "sound": "default"}, "sealed": "<blob>"}`. Answers 200 `{"ok": true}` when Apple took it. 410 when the phone isn't registered: Apple answered 410, `BadDeviceToken` or `DeviceTokenNotForTopic` (then the relay forgets it), or it was forgotten before; obid stops pushing to it until the phone hands it over again. 429 when Apple says too many to this phone, with its `Retry-After`. 503 when the relay's own sign-in with Apple is renewed too often (`TooManyProviderTokenUpdates`), or until the APNs key and `PUSH_SECRET_KEY` are set. 502 when Apple refused the push (its reason in the error) or didn't answer. |

The provider token is an ES256 JWT (`kid` the key's id, `iss` the team, `iat` now) signed with the `.p8` key. One token is shared by every isolate for 40 minutes, since Apple wants a new one at most every 20 minutes and at least every 60. It's kept in D1's `apns_provider_token` only sealed: AES-256-GCM under a key that is the HMAC of `oby-apns-token/1` under `PUSH_SECRET_KEY`, with the key's id and team as associated data. It's made again after Apple refuses it.

### What the relay can and can't see

- **What it keeps** (D1): each phone's `pushId`, APNs token, topic and `sandbox` (`push_devices`), and how many there are (`push_device_count`). No time, no address, no Mac, no account, and no secret. The sealed provider token above is the only other thing kept.
- **The rate limits' counters** (`rate_limits`, keys `push:<window>:…`) count per window. Each window is a whole minute, hour or UTC day, so a row says which minute or day it counts, never when a push or registration came. The sweep removes each one within a minute of its window's end. Registrations are counted by client and network address, by token hash for checks, and by `pushId` for re-registrations. Pushes are counted by `pushId` and by token hash. No row links an address to a `pushId`.
- **What passes through:** the sealed blob, which the relay can't open (obid seals it to the phone's own key); `collapse`, `urgent`, `at` and the `pushId`; and the address the call came from, which Cloudflare sees. Pushes carry no Mac key and no installation, only a MAC under the `pushId`'s `pushKey`, so the relay can't tell which Mac sent a push.
- **What's logged:** a push Apple didn't take, or a token check that failed for a reason other than a bad token, logs Apple's status and reason (`push: APNs 410 Unregistered, phone forgotten`). Nothing else of a push or a registration is logged: never the token, the secret, the `pushKey`, the `pushId`, `collapse` or the blob (`test/push.test.ts` checks every log line). **Invocation logs are off** for the whole control plane (`observability.logs.invocation_logs: false` in `wrangler.jsonc`, at the top and in `env.deskree`). They would record every request's URL, headers and address, and so tie a Mac's signed calls (its `Oby-Key`) to the pushes it sends from the same address.
- **Apple** sees what it always does: the token, the topic, the fixed alert, the sealed blob and the collapse id, plus one background push per new token.
- **What it can't do:** read an alert; learn which Mac or account a phone belongs to; push without the phone's `pushKey`; list phones (ids are 144 random bits, and a wrong id and a wrong MAC or secret get the same 401). obid can push but can't re-register or forget a phone.
- **What's left:**
  - Whoever holds `PUSH_SECRET_KEY` (the instance's operator) can make any phone's `pushKey` and push to it. They can't seal an alert the phone opens, so the phone shows only the fixed words.
  - Anyone who learns a phone's APNs token can register it for themselves. That moves the token off the phone's own `pushId` until the phone registers again. The token goes only to Apple and the relay.
  - A push replayed within its 5 minutes reaches the phone again, which drops it by the sealed alert's id.
  - Junk registrations need real tokens of this app, and stay until `MAX_PUSH_DEVICES`. The rates bound how fast they come.

### Setting it up (the owner's step)

Deploying is the owner's; nothing here is in git.

1. **Make the APNs key.** In Certificates, Identifiers & Profiles on Deskree's team: Keys › **+**, name it "Oby push relay", tick **Apple Push Notifications service (APNs)** › Configure: environment **Sandbox & Production**, and limit it to the topic `com.deskree.oby.ios` when Apple offers that. Register, then **Download** `AuthKey_<KEY ID>.p8` (Apple lets you download it once). Note its **Key ID**, and the **Team ID** from Membership details.
2. **Set the four secrets**, in `cloud/control-plane`, each read from stdin so none lands in your shell history:
   ```sh
   pnpm exec wrangler secret put APNS_KEY --env deskree < ~/Downloads/AuthKey_XXXXXXXXXX.p8
   pnpm exec wrangler secret put APNS_KEY_ID --env deskree
   pnpm exec wrangler secret put APNS_TEAM_ID --env deskree
   openssl rand -base64 32 | pnpm exec wrangler secret put PUSH_SECRET_KEY --env deskree
   ```
   Then move the `.p8` into your password manager and delete it from Downloads. `PUSH_SECRET_KEY` is in Cloudflare alone, and nobody needs to know it.
3. **Migrate and deploy:** `pnpm migrate:remote --env deskree` (it adds `push_devices`, `push_device_count` and `apns_provider_token`), then `pnpm run deploy --env deskree`. The deploy also turns invocation logs off.
4. **Check it:** `curl -s -X POST https://oby-control.deskree.com/v1/push -H 'Content-Type: application/json' -d '{}'` answers `{"error":"pushId is the phone's pushId."}` (422). `Pushes aren't set up on this control plane.` (503) means one of the four secrets is missing.

Rotating the APNs key changes nothing on the phones: make a new key, put the three `APNS_*` secrets again, then revoke the old key. `PUSH_SECRET_KEY` isn't rotated: a new one makes every phone's secret and `pushKey` wrong (401), and each must register anew and hand obid its new `pushId` and `pushKey`. Another instance sets its own app's bundle id in its environment's `APNS_TOPICS` and its own team's key.

## On the Mac

- **`cloudflared` ships inside a full build of Oby.app** (`Contents/MacOS/cloudflared`; the launch build leaves it out, [release.md, Full builds](release.md#full-builds)): the "Bundle cloudflared" build phase runs `apps/mac/app/Tools/bundle-cloudflared.sh`, which downloads the pinned release once into `~/Library/Caches/com.deskree.oby/cloudflared/`, checks its SHA-256, makes a universal binary and signs it with the app's identity and the hardened runtime, so it's notarized with the app. `apps/mac/tests/RemoteAccessTests.swift` checks a full build has it, obi-vm and the web app, and the launch build none of them, and that the bundled binary is signed, universal and the pinned version. To move to a newer cloudflared, change `VERSION` and the two checksums in the script.
- **Settings › Remote access** (`apps/mac/app/RemoteAccess.swift`, `apps/mac/app/Settings.swift`): Sangam's Remote page: an **Oby account** card, then **Remote access** ("Reach this Mac from anywhere", the hostname, the status, Forget This Mac). While this Mac has no hostname (never set up, forgotten, or away 30 days), the account card holds the sign-in buttons in each provider's colours, Google's white and Apple's black, only those `GET /v1/auth` lists (both when it doesn't answer), and the switch waits; either signs in in the system's web sheet, then registers and turns remote access on. Set up, the card shows "Signed in with Google · deskree.com". Oby makes this Mac's key: in the Secure Enclave when the Mac has one (the keychain keeps only the enclave's wrapped key, which no other Mac can use; if the enclave can't make one, that's an error, never a quiet step down), else a software P-256 key in the keychain, on Macs with no Secure Enclave. If the control plane doesn't know the key (410), it starts a sign-in with the provider the user picked, shows its page (`WebSignIn`), checks the sheet came back to `oby-signin://done` with the same state and a code, and registers this Mac by that key with it (the name from Sharing settings); a key it knows skips all that. Then it asks for its endpoint and hands the tunnel's run token to obid (`PUT /v1/obid/tunnel`, docs/runner-api.md, "obid only: the tunnel"), which keeps it in its vault and runs `cloudflared` as its own child with the token in a private file, never in an argument or the environment. The hostname and obid's tunnel status show there. The tunnel restarts by itself when it drops, waiting a little longer each time, and keeps running when the app quits: it's obid's, and obid runs from login. A token an older Oby kept in the app's keychain goes to obid once at launch, and the app's copy is deleted. Remote access needs This Mac in Settings › Runner: the tunnel reaches obid's gateway on this Mac.
- **Off** stops obid's tunnel (`DELETE /v1/obid/tunnel`) and keeps the hostname for 3 days (Settings says so: after 3 days off, asleep or offline, the Mac gets a new hostname when it's back, and the iPhone pairs again); **Forget This Mac** removes the installation with a call signed by the key (its tunnel and hostname go, and the hostname never answers again), then deletes the key and the token here.
- **Checking in:** with a hostname, on or off, Oby makes a signed call at launch and once a day, so the installation isn't swept. When the tunnel was reclaimed (the check-in sees no endpoint, or another hostname), Oby makes a new endpoint at once if remote access is on, with no sign-in: the installation is still there; the iPhone pairs again for the new hostname. A Mac Oby hasn't run on for 30 days loses its installation: at its next check-in (410) remote access turns off and Settings says to sign in again, with the two buttons. A check-in never opens a sign-in sheet by itself.
- **What's kept where:** the keychain, service `com.deskree.oby.remote`: `installation-key` (`se:` and the enclave's wrapped key, or `sw:` and a software key). The tunnel's run token is in obid's vault (`oby.remote.tunnel`). The defaults: `remote.hostname`, `remote.enabled`. The user's vault (Settings › Vault) isn't involved. At launch Oby deletes what the email sign-in left (the keychain's `control-plane-session` and `installation-credential`, the defaults `remote.email`, `remote.installation`, `remote.clientID`).

## On the iPhone

Settings › Your Mac (`apps/ios/app/ConnectScreen.swift`) links the Mac: its name (as Sharing settings call it) and its hostname. Pairing by QR code comes with the gateway (#284) and carries the same two fields (`MacLink`'s JSON, `{"name", "hostname"}`); until then they're typed. Linked, the iPhone talks to the Mac instead of the runner's address:

- **At home,** it looks for the Mac's gateway with Bonjour (`_oby._tcp`, the service named after the Mac), resolves its address and talks to it on the local network. `apps/ios/app/Info.plist` declares the service and the local-network use.
- **Away,** it goes through the tunnel, `https://<hostname>`.

The switch between the two keeps the conversation: only the connection is made anew (`apps/shared/RemoteRoute.swift` picks the route; `Phone.base` is where the calls go). Unlink, and the iPhone is back on the runner.

## Limits and what's next

- The gateway on `127.0.0.1:8810` is #284: until it exists, the tunnel is up and routes to nothing (Cloudflare answers 502). Device tokens and the route allowlist are its job; the control plane only makes the tunnel.
- The Mac staying awake is #286. Pushes go through the sealed relay ([Pushes](#pushes)) once the owner sets its secrets; obid's side and the iPhone's are their own pieces of work. End-to-end encryption between the devices and the Mac is #287; the runner on the Mac is #283 and #288.
- Company Obyspheres gated by a Workspace domain ("only @deskree.com") read `users.workspace_domain` (Google's `hd`) and nothing else: `email_domain` never grants membership.
- App Attest, or another check that a key belongs to a real Oby, would sit on top of the key (see "Abuse control" above).
